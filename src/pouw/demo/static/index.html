Limitations

Small inner dimension (k)

needs research

Attention multiplies over the head size, 128 values. The noise only pays off when k is at least 16 times the noise rank: 2,048 here.

KV cache commitment

needs research

Attention reads keys and values cached from earlier tokens. They change every step, and nothing commits to them.

Fused attention kernels (FlashAttention)

needs research

Both attention multiplies and the softmax run in one kernel that never stores the full product. Noise and hashing would have to move inside it.

Floating-point GEMMs

needs research

The verifier recomputes a tile and must get the same bits. Integer math is exact; fp8 and bf16 results depend on rounding order. In this checkpoint, every down_proj, q/k/v_proj in layers 0–15, and lm_head are fp8 or bf16.

Small batches (m < 1,024)

measured

Batches under 1,024 tokens use the baseline GEMM. In a decode-heavy H100 run (256-token prompts, 512-token answers), only 40% of int7 GEMM work used the noisy GEMM.

Non-GEMM operations

by design

Softmax, normalization, activations, and embedding lookups aren't matrix multiplies, and they're a small share of the compute.

Assumptions

  1. Hardness of noisy GEMM (the paper's Assumption 6.4). Producing the hashed running sums of the noised matrices costs as much as the full multiply, whatever the inputs. The paper states this as a conjecture.
  2. Noise distribution. The paper assumes uniformly random noise over a finite field. Upstream uses sparse and small-integer noise on plain integers. Nobody has checked that the argument carries over.
  3. BLAKE3 as a random oracle. The only way to find a tile whose hash falls below the target is to compute tiles and try them.
  4. Unpredictable challenges. Each job carries 32 random bytes the prover can't predict, and jobs expire after 15 s.
  5. Declared model. The verifier holds the checkpoint the prover should run. Proofs show its weights were used, not that it's the only model on the GPU.
  6. Known unproven work. To read proven work as a share of capacity, the verifier needs the GPU's peak rate and an estimate of the work that earns no proofs.

Future work

Hardware attestation (H100 CC mode)

Issue a GPU's job only after it signs the verifier's nonce with its device key. On this run's pod, CC mode was off, and the driver's certificate and attestation report calls both failed.

Delay-based location verification

Landmarks time signed nonce replies to bound a GPU's distance. The GPU ID in the job ties proven work to that GPU. Relays and extracted device keys defeat it.

Mixture of experts (MoE)

Upstream can prove expert layers, but this verifier rejects those proofs. It needs to match each proof to an expert's weights.

Tensor parallelism

When a model is split across GPUs, each holds part of every weight matrix. The verifier must match proofs to those parts.

GPU-side Merkle proofs

Take proof paths from the Merkle trees the GPU already builds, so proofs don't pause inference.

Sybil resistance for GPU IDs

Each new ID costs the verifier a pass of weight hashing, so it needs a cap before untrusted provers connect.
